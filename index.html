EMERGENCY BROADCAST
    Field manual & prototype rules

    Your rules. Health: 0–20. Energy: 0–20. Movement costs 1 energy. Special attacks cost 2 energy. Claw attacks and feeding cost 0 energy. Healing restores up to 4 health per action at a cost of 1 energy per health point. If fewer than 4 health points are needed to reach 20, restore and charge only that amount. At full health and energy, choose a new ability. At 0 health, you die. Below 6 energy, you automatically pursue and attack the nearest food source. At 0 energy, lose 1 health at the end of every turn.

    First-version balance. Start at 15 health and 14 energy. Feeding and feeding attacks are one action. Feed on a civilian, police officer, soldier, or rival monster in your territory: deal exactly 1 health damage and restore 5 energy. Feeding has no energy cost and restores energy only. Civilians, police, and soldiers each have 1 health. The target is consumed when its health reaches zero. Tanks cannot be eaten. Helicopters and scientists cannot be targeted by a feeding attack. Claws deal 2 damage and cannot hit helicopters or scientists. Atomic roar deals exactly 3 health damage to every enemy in the territory and destroys all civilian groups there. Helicopters and scientists can suffer only special attacks; a rival’s remains restore 2 health and 4 energy. Your monster gets two actions per turn, or three after the Third action evolution. After your final action, phases run in order: monsters, civilians, police, military, then scientists. Each move is animated across the board for two seconds; units moving in the same phase travel together. Sound effects accompany movement; use Sound on/off to mute. City fire growth follows the movement phases. Police deal 1 damage at range 0 when sharing the monster hex. Armored hide subtracts 1 from each incoming hit, with a minimum of 0 damage. Otherwise police take one action per turn to fight fires or rescue civilians; they prioritize fires, then civilian groups. Rival monsters take two actions, moving toward you or attacking. The initial rival has 6 health and 2 attack damage. A new rival enters at the map edge on turns 20, 40, 60, and every 20 turns thereafter. Each new tier has 10% more maximum health and attack damage than the preceding tier, rounded to one decimal; tier 1 has 6.6 health and 2.2 attack damage. Scientists have 3 health and attack within range 1. At game start, the scientist rolls 1d6 for the first weapon’s total damage. Every new weapon randomly splits its rolled total between health and energy, including all damage to one meter. Both amounts remain fixed for that weapon. No military starts on the map. Reinforcements enter at an edge at the start of each turn: 0 on turns 1–10, 1 on 11–20, 2 on 21–30, increasing by 1 every ten turns. A d6 chooses infantry on 1–3, armor on 4–5, helicopters on 6. Each military unit gets one action per turn: attack if in range, otherwise advance one territory. Infantry: 1 health / range 0 / 2 damage. Armor: 3 health / range 0 / 3 damage. Helicopters: 2 health / range 1 / 3 damage. Civilians move farther from the monster every third turn; groups reaching an edge remain there until the next civilian movement turn, when they escape. New games start with three police officers in randomly chosen city territories. At the start of every turn, each territory rolls 2d6: 7 adds one civilian group; other results add nothing. Separately, one citywide d6 roll of 1 or 2 adds one police officer in a random territory; 3–6 adds none. Civilians that start with police receive an extra movement opportunity during the civilian phase, limited to one move per turn. A scientist enters at an edge every 15 turns, even if another scientist is present. It has two actions per turn: move, attack within range 1, or retreat. Its arrival has a 50% chance of selecting the highest-damage discovered weapon and a 50% chance of a new invention; if none is known, it invents one. After three shots it flies toward an edge, exits, and returns on a later turn with a newly rolled weapon. All recovery is capped. No passive energy loss except the scientist.

    Arachnoid. Movement causes no city damage and leaves a 10-HP web. Webs block civilian, police, infantry and tank movement until destroyed. Trapped civilians deal 1 web damage per group on a movement opportunity; police deal 1, soldiers 2 and tanks 3 per action spent clearing webs. Flying units and monsters ignore ordinary webs. Flaming Web Shooter kills 1 priority target, evolving to 2 and then 3 distinct targets; it costs 2 energy, causes 30% city damage, ignites the sector and blocks ground movement through the next enemy movement phase, then expires. Scientist, rival, aircraft, then random ground targets are prioritized. Double Claw kills two ground targets. Feeding excludes armor and aircraft.
    Mechanicus. Siege Walker feeds on city structures: 10% damage and +5 energy, with no feeding energy cost. Rubble cannot be consumed. Death Ray costs 2 energy per action and deals 3 health damage per strike. Death Ray does not start fires; existing fires remain until extinguished or the sector becomes rubble. Double Death Ray and then Triple Death Ray grant two and three strikes, targeting scientists, rivals, helicopters, then random ground units. Movement retains the ordinary 10% city damage. Target selection applies to feeding and claw attacks.
    Balrog. Movement causes 10% damage and ignites the destination, unless it is rubble. Flaming Whip costs 2 energy and deals 3 damage per strike. Double Strike and then Triple Strike grant two and three strikes for each claw or whip action. Eligible targets are chosen in order: scientist, rival monster, helicopter, then random civilians, police, infantry or armor. Claws cannot hit scientists or helicopters. Remaining strikes continue against ground units. Double Feeding bites a selected rival twice if it survives the first bite.
    Monsters. New rampage opens monster selection. Funguloid automatically feeds on arrival without an extra action or extra feeding damage; walking still causes 10% damage. Its Splat Attack kills one unit, with Double splat and then Triple splat evolutions increasing the target limit to two and three. Splat causes 10%, 20%, or 30% city damage for its current tier, costs 2 energy, and supports Extended range. Civilians are targeted first. Every claw attack causes 10% city damage.
    Scoring. Earn 1 point for every percentage point of damage across all city sectors, including fire damage. Each sector contributes at most 100 points. Defeating a civilian earns 10 points; a police officer or soldier 20; a tank or helicopter 30; a rival monster or scientist 50. Units award points only when defeated, whether by feeding, claws, or a special attack. Escaping civilians and scientists leaving to rearm earn no points. New rampages start at zero. Existing saves retain their city damage score; unit points are tracked from this update onward.
    Destruction. Entering a territory, feeding, or waiting adds 10% damage; a special attack adds 30% and ignites the territory, even if it has no targets. Burning territories gain 5% damage at the end of each turn until they reach 100% and stop burning. After that increase, each territory still burning rolls independently for each neighbor: damage divided by 10 gives the percentage chance of ignition (60% damage = 6%). Newly ignited territories spread and take fire damage starting on the next turn. A police officer entering or spawning in a burning territory extinguishes it. The overall city damage percentage is the average damage across all 61 hexes and is shown above the map. At 100% damage, the fire goes out and the hex becomes permanent brown rubble. Rubble cannot be repaired, cleared, or reignited. Damage caps at 100%. Civilians cannot enter or leave rubble unless a police officer is present in that rubble territory. Police open both entry and exit there. Military and police movement is unaffected. The red monster marks your position.
    Evolution & navigation. Evolution is optional and free, once per visit to full health and energy; spend energy before evolving again. Choose armor, stronger claws, +2 energy per feeding target, Double feeding (up to two distinct units in one feeding action), Extended range (target your sector or one adjacent sector with the special attack), or Third action (one additional action now and three actions per turn thereafter). Each feeding target restores energy separately. With Extended range, select Aim atomic roar to change the pointer to an aiming reticle, then click your own or one adjacent highlighted hex to fire. Cancel aiming to return to movement. Special attacks still cost 1 energy and deal exactly 3 damage to every unit in the selected sector, igniting it. A pulse at the map edge signals berserk mode: the next action follows the shortest available route to food. Movement at zero energy is permitted during berserk turns. This is a stylized game board based on central Tokyo districts, not a navigational map. No roads or subway layers.
